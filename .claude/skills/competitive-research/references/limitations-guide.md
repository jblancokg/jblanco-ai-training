# Limitations Guide

Every piece of competitive research has limitations. Acknowledging them honestly makes the research more useful, not less. Readers trust findings that are upfront about what they do not know.

## Standard Limitations to Always Include

Include these in every research deliverable's "Limitations" section, adapted to the specifics:

### Temporal Snapshot
Research reflects a point in time. Competitors update pricing, ship features, pivot strategy, and hire/fire regularly. State the date range of research and recommend a refresh cadence based on how fast the market moves.

Example:
> This research was conducted between [start date] and [end date]. Competitive landscapes in [market] shift frequently. Recommend refreshing pricing and feature data quarterly, and doing a full competitive review annually.

### Source Access Constraints
Not all information is publicly available. Be explicit about what you could not access:

- Gated or paywalled analyst reports
- Enterprise-only pricing (requires sales contact)
- Private companies with limited public financial data
- Products requiring an account or trial to evaluate features
- Regions or languages not covered in the research

Example:
> [Competitor]'s enterprise pricing requires a sales conversation and is not included. Feature analysis is based on public documentation and may not reflect capabilities available only to enterprise customers.

### Information Asymmetry
You will inevitably know more about some competitors than others. This is usually because some companies are more transparent, not because they are better.

Example:
> [Competitor A] publishes detailed documentation and public pricing, giving us high confidence in those sections. [Competitor B] has minimal public information; findings rely more heavily on third-party reviews and should be verified through direct evaluation.

### Web Search Limitations
Web search does not surface everything. Some relevant information may exist behind paywalls, in languages not searched, or on pages that do not rank well.

Example:
> Research was conducted via English-language web search. Competitors with stronger presence in non-English markets may be underrepresented.

### No Product Testing
Unless you actually used the products, note that findings are based on public claims and third-party reports, not hands-on evaluation.

Example:
> Feature assessments are based on official documentation and user reviews, not direct product testing. Actual user experience may differ from documented capabilities.

## Situational Limitations

Include these when they apply:

### Startup / Pre-Revenue Competitors
Early-stage companies change rapidly. What they say on their website today may not reflect the product in three months.

### Rapidly Evolving Markets
In markets with frequent product launches and pivots (AI tools being a prime current example), research has a shorter shelf life. Flag this prominently.

### Biased Source Pool
If most available information comes from the competitors themselves (limited third-party coverage), note the potential for self-serving framing.

### Sample Size in Reviews
"4.5 stars on G2" means very different things with 15 reviews vs. 1,500 reviews. Always note review count alongside ratings.

## How to Write Limitations

Do:
- Be specific about what is limited and why
- Suggest what the reader could do to fill the gap (e.g., "request a demo," "check pricing directly")
- Place limitations near the relevant findings, not just in a footnote at the end
- Frame limitations as useful information, not apologies

Do not:
- Use limitations as a blanket disclaimer that undermines the entire research
- Be so hedged that the reader cannot act on anything
- Bury critical limitations in fine print
- Skip limitations because "it should be obvious"

## Limitation Statement Template

For the deliverable's limitations section:

```markdown
## Limitations and Caveats

**Research window:** [Date range]. Findings reflect the competitive landscape as of this period.

**Source constraints:**
- [Specific things you could not access or verify]

**Methodology notes:**
- [How research was conducted and any inherent limits of that approach]

**Recommended next steps to fill gaps:**
- [Specific actions: request demos, contact sales, run a trial, etc.]
```
